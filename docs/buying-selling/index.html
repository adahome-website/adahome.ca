Financing, explained

Soft approval vs. firm pre-approval

Soft approval (pre-qualification)

A rough estimate

  • Based on what you tell the lender, often with a soft credit check
  • Income, debts and down payment are not yet verified
  • Useful for budgeting, but it can change once documents are reviewed

Firm pre-approval

What we need before shopping

  • The lender has reviewed your documents: income, credit, debts and down payment
  • Usually comes with a rate hold for a set period
  • Tells us your real price range, including monthly costs like condo fees
Why I insist on a firm pre-approval: condo fees and property taxes count against how much a lender will approve. A soft approval can look fine until fees are added, so a firm pre-approval protects you from falling in love with a home you can't finance, and from risking your deposit.

Until the keys are in your hand

Hold off on big life changes before closing.

Your financing isn't final until your mortgage funds on closing day. Lenders can re-check your credit, income and debts right up to the end, so a big change can put the whole deal at risk.

Please wait until after closing for

A new car or other vehicle

Even a small monthly payment can shrink what a lender will approve.

A job change or new job

Lenders often re-verify employment before funding. A new role, a probation period or a switch to self-employment can stall or sink approval.

New credit cards or loans

Opening new credit, or financing furniture and appliances, changes your credit and debt ratios.

Big withdrawals or unexplained deposits

Your down payment funds need a clear paper trail. Moving money around can raise questions.

Co-signing for someone else

Their debt counts as yours in the lender's eyes.

Missed or late payments

Keep every bill current. A single missed payment can lower your credit score.

The simple rule: if it involves your job, your credit or a large amount of money, call me and your mortgage specialist before you do it.
