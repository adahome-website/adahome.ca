Moving from another province

Your first-90-days checklist

Coming from Ontario, BC or anywhere else in Canada? The move is simpler than you think, and buying here usually costs less up front.

Health card

Register for Alberta Health Care within 3 months of moving. Coverage starts on the first day of the third month after you arrive, and your old province covers you until then.

Licence and vehicle

Swap your driver's licence and register your vehicle at any Alberta registry agent soon after you move. You'll also need insurance from an Alberta insurer.

No land transfer tax

Alberta charges small Land Titles registration fees instead. If you're coming from Ontario or BC, that alone can save you thousands at closing.

Taxes

There's no provincial sales tax, just the 5% GST. For income tax, you file as a resident of the province you lived in on December 31.

Selling back home?

I'll line up your Calgary possession date with your sale, and connect you with a mortgage broker about bridge financing if the dates don't match.

Utilities

In Alberta you choose your own electricity and natural gas retailer. Before possession, I'll send you a list of what to set up and when.

Moving from another country

Settling in, then buying

Most of my newcomer clients do things in this order. You don't have to rush the house; getting these basics done first makes the mortgage much easier.

  1. 01The first weeks

    Get your Social Insurance Number, open a Canadian bank account, and apply for Alberta Health Care within 3 months. Bring your immigration document (PR card, work or study permit) to a registry agent.

  2. 02Driving

    Depending on where your licence is from, you may be able to exchange it directly or you'll need to take Alberta's tests. Check with a registry agent early, as road tests can book up.

  3. 03Start building credit

    Get a Canadian credit card right away, use it lightly and pay it in full every month. Even a few months of history helps when you apply for a mortgage.

  4. 04Talk to a mortgage broker

    Permanent residents can get an insured mortgage with as little as 5% down. New to Canadian credit? Lenders may accept a credit report and statements from back home.

  5. 05Move your down payment early

    Lenders need to see where your down payment came from. Transfer it to Canada well before you buy and keep every statement, so the paper trail is clean.

  6. 06Rent first, if it helps

    Many newcomers rent for 6 to 12 months to learn the city. I'm happy to help you pick the right area for that too, so you're already where you want to buy.

The big question

Can I buy without PR or citizenship?

Permanent residents can buy just like citizens. Most other non-Canadians can't buy a home in Calgary right now, but there are important exceptions, and the rule is set to end on January 1, 2027.

The rule, in plain language

Canada's Prohibition on the Purchase of Residential Property by Non-Canadians Act has been in force since January 1, 2023. It's currently set to end on January 1, 2027. As of October 2026 the federal government is reviewing it and hasn't said whether it will end, be extended or change. I'll update this page as soon as it's decided.

Breaking the rule can mean a fine of up to $10,000, and a court can order the home sold.

Who can still buy

  • Canadian citizens, permanent residents and people registered under the Indian Act
  • Work permit holders with at least 183 days left on their permit, buying one home
  • International students who meet tax-filing and time-in-Canada conditions, buying one home of $500,000 or less
  • Protected persons, and refugee claimants whose claim has been referred for a hearing
  • Anyone buying together with a spouse or common-law partner who is a citizen, PR or otherwise exempt

What it covers

Houses with up to 3 units, semi-detached homes, townhouses and condos in Canada's cities and larger towns, which includes Calgary.

Homes outside those areas, and buildings with 4 or more units, aren't covered. Renting is always allowed.
